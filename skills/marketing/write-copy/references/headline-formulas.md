# Headline Formulas

## Outcome-Focused

**{Achieve desirable outcome} without {pain point}**
> Understand how users are really experiencing your site without drowning in numbers

**{Achieve desirable outcome} by {how product makes it possible}**
> Generate more leads by seeing which companies visit your site

**Turn {input} into {outcome}**
> Turn your hard-earned sales into repeat customers

**[Achieve outcome] in [timeframe]**
> Get your tax refund in 10 days

---

## Problem-Focused

**Never {unpleasant event} again**
> Never miss a sales opportunity again

**{Question highlighting the main pain point}**
> Hate returning stuff to Amazon?

**Stop [pain]. Start [pleasure].**
> Stop chasing invoices. Start getting paid on time.

---

## Audience-Focused

**{Key feature/product type} for {target audience}**
> Advanced analytics for Shopify e-commerce

**{Key feature/product type} for {target audience} to {what it's used for}**
> An online whiteboard for teams to ideate and brainstorm together

**You don't have to {skills or resources} to {achieve desirable outcome}**
> With Ahrefs, you don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher and get more traffic

---

## Differentiation-Focused

**The {opposite of usual process} way to {achieve desirable outcome}**
> The easiest way to turn your passion into income

**The [category] that [key differentiator]**
> The CRM that updates itself

---

## Proof-Focused

**[Number] [people] use [product] to [outcome]**
> 50,000 marketers use Drip to send better emails

**{Key benefit of your product}**
> Sound clear in online meetings

## Objection-to-Hook Conversion

The hooks that test best are usually built from the audience's strongest *no*, not the brand's strongest *yes*. This subsection converts customer objections — collected from sales calls, churn interviews, support tickets, review mining, or VoC research — into headline candidates.

### The Conversion Move

A real audience objection has three parts:

1. **The frame** the audience uses to refuse ("too expensive", "no time", "won't work for me", "I already have one", "I tried that and it didn't work")
2. **The hidden assumption** underneath it ("expensive = more than my existing tool", "no time = can't add a new workflow", "won't work = my situation is special")
3. **The proof point** that breaks the assumption, if one exists

A good objection-hook reframes the objection as the headline's value claim, so the reader's first thought is "wait, *that's* the angle?" instead of "another marketing page." It works because it earns the right to keep reading by acknowledging the resistance the reader already feels.

### Five Objection-Hook Patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Price reframe** | "Why this {price} tool is cheaper than your current {free / existing} {workflow}" | "Why this $100/mo tool is cheaper than your current free spreadsheet" |
| **Time reframe** | "How {audience} {get outcome} in {tiny window} — even when {time objection}" | "How solo founders ship in 20 minutes a day — even when calendars are full" |
| **Skepticism reframe** | "Yes, {category} usually {audience complaint}. Here's the {Number}-step difference." | "Yes, most CRMs add work. Here's the 3-step difference." |
| **Tried-that reframe** | "If {prior solution} didn't work for you, it's probably because {hidden assumption}" | "If outbound didn't work, it's probably because your list, not your script" |
| **Specificity reframe** | "{Generic claim audience hears} is wrong. The right number is {specific}." | "'Most teams save time' is wrong. The teams that save time share these 4 habits." |

### Sourcing Objections (Inputs)

Pull from real audience signal — never invent the objection:

- Sales-call notes and disqualifying reasons
- Churn interview transcripts
- Support tickets tagged "pricing", "won't work", "too complex"
- Review-mining negatives (1-2 star reviews + neutral 3-star)
- Comment sections on competitor content
- ICP research VoC section (canonical input — pull the operator's `research/icp-research.md` objection list)

If no objection signal exists in `research/icp-research.md` or the operator's own customer transcripts, **flag the gap** and surface the missing-input warning rather than fabricating objections from generic SaaS tropes.

### Hook Quality Gate (Three-Question Test, V/F/U)

Apply the existing V/F/U test to every objection-hook candidate:

- **Visual?** Can the reader picture the specific situation? ("free spreadsheet" → yes; "current process" → no)
- **Falsifiable?** Can the reframe be tested? (A specific price claim is falsifiable; "we're different" is not.)
- **Uniquely Ours?** Would a competitor sign the same hook? Objection-hooks tend to score high on U because the reframe ties to *your* mechanism — but verify with the Competitor Swap Test.

### Anti-Patterns

- **Invented objection.** A hook framed around "most tools are too complex" when no audience research surfaces that objection. Reads as marketing voice, not audience voice.
- **Objection without proof.** "Why our CRM is cheaper than free" — the headline opens a loop the page must close. If body copy cannot deliver the math, the hook burns trust on read-through.
- **Compound objections in one hook.** Stacking "price + time + skepticism" in one headline diffuses the reframe. One objection per hook; test additional objections as variant hooks.
- **Negation that nobody disputes.** "Yes, most tools don't really save time" — if the audience never thought tools *did* save time, the reframe lands flat. The objection must be one the audience genuinely holds.
