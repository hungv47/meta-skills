# Spring-Launch Campaign — Cycle 1 Evaluation

## Verdict

- Status: keep
- Confidence: medium
- Campaign: spring-launch (4-channel subscription-app launch)
- Primary metric: campaign-driven net-new subscribers = 164 vs 120 baseline
- Decision: the spring-launch campaign beat the baseline launch by +37% on campaign-driven net-new subscribers (164 vs 120) and the lift is carried by genuine driver channels — keep the 4-channel launch structure, but paid-social is underwater at a $92 CAC (≈6.1-month payback vs the ≤3-month target) and must be reworked or cut next cycle.

## What Changed This Cycle

- **Source plan-campaign artifact:** `.forsvn/artifacts/mkt/campaign-plan.md` (synthetic — D20 infrastructure proof)
- **Channel-mix change:** the Q4 baseline launch ran paid-social + organic-linkedin + email. This cycle added **content-seo** as a fourth channel (4 SEO articles) and increased organic-linkedin cadence (12 posts vs the baseline's ~6).
- **Frozen between cycles:** the product ($19/mo subscription), the primary metric definition (campaign-driven net-new subscribers), the attribution model (last-click via CRM), the audience segment.

## Diagnosis

### Likely Drivers

1. **The two organic channels carried the net-new lift at low cost.** Evidence: organic-linkedin 58 net-new at a $15.52 CAC, content-seo 41 net-new at a $24.39 CAC — together 99 of the 164 campaign-driven net-new (60%) for $1,900 of the $8,500 spend (22%). Adding content-seo as a fourth channel is the clearest delta from the baseline launch and it landed.
2. **Paid-social drove real net-new but at an unsustainable cost.** Evidence: 65 net-new — genuinely incremental reach (320,000 of the ~399,200 total) — but at a $92.31 CAC. It is a driver, not a rider; the problem is its unit economics, not its attribution.
3. **The headline conversion count (234) overstates campaign performance by 70.** Evidence: the email channel's 70 conversions came from existing trial-list users (see Channel-Mix Signals). Reading 234 instead of 164 would inflate net-new by 43% and CAC would look 30% cheaper than it is.

### Channel-Mix Signals

| Channel | Spend / Effort | Reach | Leads | Conversions | Revenue | CAC | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| paid-social | $6,000 media | 320,000 | 540 | 65 | $1,235 | $92.31 | driver |
| organic-linkedin | $900 effort (12 posts) | 48,000 | 380 | 58 | $1,102 | $15.52 | driver |
| content-seo | $1,000 effort (4 articles) | 22,000 | 290 | 41 | $779 | $24.39 | driver |
| email | $600 effort (3 sends) | 9,200 | 210 | 70 | $1,330 | $8.57 | rider |

- channel_mix_read: paid-social, organic-linkedin, and content-seo are genuine `driver` channels — each created net-new reach and demand the campaign generated. **email is a `rider`** — its 9,200 reach was the existing trial-user list, and its 70 conversions are trial users who were already inside the trial→paid funnel before the campaign launched. Last-click attribution handed email the final-touch credit, but the campaign did not create that demand.
- rider_channels_excluded: email — its 70 conversions are NOT counted toward the 164 campaign-driven net-new. Counting them would inflate net-new to 234 and drop blended CAC to a misleading $36.32. The email send is still operationally fine as a trial-conversion nudge; it is just not a campaign-acquisition channel.
- breakdown_completeness: yes — all 4 channels that received spend or effort appear in the table.

### Unit-Economics Signals

- blended_CAC: **$51.83** — fully-loaded total spend ($8,500) ÷ 164 campaign-driven net-new. This beats the baseline launch's $58.00 by ~11%.
- naive_blended_CAC_rejected: $36.32 — fully-loaded spend ÷ 234 gross conversions. Rejected: it counts the email rider's 70 conversions as campaign-driven. Reporting $36.32 would launder an honest $51.83 into a false win.
- paid_CAC: **$92.31** — paid-social spend ($6,000) ÷ 65 paid-social net-new. Reported distinct from blended CAC.
- payback_period: at ~$15.20/mo gross contribution ($19 × ~80% margin) — organic-linkedin ≈1.0 month, content-seo ≈1.6 months, blended ≈3.4 months, **paid-social ≈6.1 months**. The ≤3-month target is met by the two organic channels and missed by paid-social by ~2×.
- ltv_cac_ratio: not yet measurable — the cohort is new; revisit at the 90-day retention mark.
- revenue_attribution_note: the $3,116 first-month MRR is campaign-driven only (164 × $19); email's $1,330 is excluded as rider revenue. First-month revenue is below spend by design — subscription payback is multi-month.
- unit_economics_read: **mixed** — the campaign blended is healthy and improved on baseline, but it is carried entirely by the two cheap organic channels; paid-social alone is underwater.

### Confounders

- A concurrent brand-awareness campaign ran in weeks 3-4 — it may have lifted organic-linkedin engagement and branded search; the organic-channel net-new is plausibly a slight upper bound.
- Mild spring seasonal uptick for productivity apps — not quantified; flagged.
- No price or promo change during the window.
- Last-click attribution over-credits the email last touch and under-credits content-seo (a top-of-funnel channel) — content-seo's true contribution is plausibly higher than the 41 last-click conversions show.
- Possible channel cannibalization between paid-social and organic-linkedin (same ICP, overlapping feed) — unquantified.

## Next Cycle Recommendation

- Keep: the 4-channel launch structure; organic-linkedin + content-seo as the economic engine of the launch (1.0 and 1.6-month paybacks); the addition of content-seo as a channel
- Discard: paid-social at its current $6,000 spend and $92 CAC — underwater at ~6.1-month payback against a ≤3-month target
- Watch: blended CAC — $51.83 is a single-cycle figure; cycle 2-3 must confirm it is not an artifact, especially with the rider correctly excluded
- Route next work to: plan-campaign --rev=2 with hypothesis: "paid-social CAC underwater at $92 (≈6.1-month payback vs ≤3-month target) — cut paid-social budget by ~60% and reallocate to organic-linkedin + content-seo (1.0-1.6-month paybacks); before fully cutting paid-social, test whether a smaller, tighter-targeted spend can reach ≤3-month payback; hold the rest of the channel mix and the offer constant"

## Learning Promotion

- Promote to `learnings.md`: yes
- Lesson: For the subscription-app ICP, an email send to the existing trial-user list is a campaign `rider`, not a `driver` — its conversions come from trial users already inside the trial→paid funnel, so they must be excluded from campaign-driven net-new. Counting them inflated net-new by 43% (234 vs 164) and laundered blended CAC from an honest $51.83 to a misleading $36.32.
- Expiry / caveat: scoped to launch campaigns that include a warm-list email channel for this ICP. The rider classification is structural (it does not depend on the exact numbers), but the magnitude of the distortion is a single synthetic cycle — confirm on a cycle 3 replication. Retest if the email channel is re-pointed at a cold list (a cold-list email send could be a genuine driver) or if the attribution model changes off last-click.
